How I build it

Four layers, one job each. The failure mode with four tools is two of them doing the same job badly, so the boundaries come first.

LayerIts one job
SourcingFind candidates and send sequences
GateEnrich, score, and decide who is contactable
RecordEvery cleared company, and everything that happens to it
CaptureNotes land on the record without anyone typing

The rules underneath matter more than the tools, which are swappable:

  • A fit score with a floor. Five criteria, twenty points each. Above the line gets contacted. Below it stays in the gate, uncontacted, as suppression memory so nobody works it twice.
  • A hard size gate before scoring, verified against enrichment data rather than a sourcing tool's own confidence number. Those read job titles and hiring recency, and they will hand you enterprise logos in a campaign built for 150-person companies.
  • A deal exists when a human replies. Everything before that is a company status. Cold reply rates run 3–6%, so counting sends as pipeline buries the real thing under silence.
  • Attribution set at record creation and never edited. The sourced-by field doubles as the commission register, so compensation questions never depend on anyone's memory.
  • Automation is a purchase. Manual export holds until volume clears roughly 500 records a week. Below that, the round trip costs fifteen minutes and buys you a system you can still see into.

Sender reputation gets treated as a budget, because it only gets spent once. Fresh inboxes warm on a ramp before real volume, sending caps hold per inbox per day with rotation ahead of any increase, and sequences stop at four touches. Every message opens with something true and specific about the buyer's business — the AI drafts the research brief, a person writes the line.

The economics conversation

When price is the blocker, the pitch is usually built on the wrong number. A buyer keeps the margin on an incremental sale, so a deck that compares your subscription to incremental revenue can overstate your value by roughly half.

I rebuild it the way the buyer does it: contribution margin by segment, break-even by tier stated as a number they can check in their head, and a holdout test designed so the lift you claim is the lift you caused. Buyers know their own margins cold. Showing up with their math builds trust before the demo starts.
